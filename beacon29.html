In this Beacon, you won't learn a new tool.

📌 **What kind of proje

Imagine you want to create a short personal newsletter — something you could send to friends, share online, or just keep for yourself.

Here's what goes into it:

  • A short written intro (your words, AI-polished)
  • An image that matches your topic
  • A short AI-generated audio clip — like a spoken intro

That's three tools. Three skills. One project.

📌 **The tools you'll u

You've already met all of them:

✍️ Text → Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude

🎨 Image → Leonardo AI or any image tool from the course

🔊 Audio → ElevenLabs

No new sign-ups. No new interfaces. Just tools you already know.

🧪 Try it now

Screens and menus may look slightly different — that's normal. Just explore and you'll find your way. 😊

You'll build your mini-newsletter in three steps. Take your time — there's no rush.

Step 1 – Write your intro

Pick a topic that feels personal to you. Here are some ideas:

  • Something I learned this month
  • A place I'd love to visit
  • A book or film that stuck with me
  • My morning routine
  • A small win I'm proud of
  • A skill I want to learn
  • How I use technology in daily life

"Open your favourite AI writing tool from the course and paste this prompt…”

"Write a short, warm newsletter intro (around 80 words) on this topic: [your topic]. Friendly tone, like writing to a friend. First person."

Copy the result. You'll use it in Step 3.

Step 2 – Create your image (Leonardo AI)

Go to https://app.leonardo.ai

Use this ready-made prompt — or adapt it to match your topic:

"A warm, editorial-style illustration for a personal newsletter about [your topic]. Soft colors, cozy atmosphere, no text."

Download the image when you're happy with it.

Step 3 – Record your audio intro (ElevenLabs)

Go to https://elevenlabs.io

Paste the text you got from Step 1 into the text box.

Choose a voice you like, then click Generate.

Listen back. Does it sound like something you'd actually want to hear?

If yes — you're done. ✅

👀 What to notice

Look at what you just made.

You started with an idea — and ended up with a piece of writing, a visual, and a voice.

Each tool did one thing. But together, they made something complete.

Notice how the tools didn't fight each other — the text fed the audio, the topic shaped the image. That's not a coincidence. That's what it looks like when you're in control.

The bigger insight: you were the creative director the whole time.

💡 Good to know

This kind of "AI workflow" — where one tool's output becomes another tool's input — is how a lot of real creative work gets done today.

You don't need to use every tool every time. Some projects only need one. Some need three.

The point is: you now know how to choose.
