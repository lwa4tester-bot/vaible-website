🛠️Meet the
tool

You've been talking to AI so far.

An agent is AI that works for you.

Instead of answering one question at a time, an agent takes a goal — and figures out the steps to get there on its own.

Think of it like this: instead of asking "how do I plan a trip to Lisbon?" and then manually following every step yourself — an agent could research options, build an itinerary, draft a packing list, and write a summary. All from one instruction.

You don't need special software for this. Claude and ChatGPT can both act as basic agents — today, on the free plan.

🧭**One goal,
many steps**

Here's what makes an agent different:

Normal AI chat:

You ask → AI answers → You ask again → AI answers again

Agent-style thinking:

You give a goal → AI breaks it into steps → AI works through them → AI delivers a result

You're still in charge. The AI just does more of the legwork.

This is sometimes called agentic behaviour — and it's already built into Claude and ChatGPT. You just need to know how to trigger it.

💬**How to
trigger agent mode**

You don't need to press a special button. The trick is how you phrase your request.

Instead of asking one narrow question, give the AI a broader goal with context — and ask it to think through the steps itself.

Here's the pattern:

"I want to [goal]. Please break this down into steps, work through each one, and give me a final result."

That's it. The AI will plan its own approach, execute it step by step, and show you the work.

Some tools — like Claude's Projects feature or ChatGPT's memory settings — let you build on this over time. But even in a single conversation, you can experience what agent-style AI feels like.

🧪Try it
now

Screens and menus may look slightly different — that's normal. Just explore and you'll find your way. 😊

You're going to give Claude and ChatGPT the same agent-style goal — and see how each one approaches it.

Step 1 — Try it in Claude

Go to → claude.ai

Start a new chat and paste this prompt:

"I want to start learning Spanish from scratch. I have 10 minutes a day. Please break this into a realistic 4-week plan: outline each week's focus, suggest one free resource for each week, and finish with a motivational note to keep me going."

Watch how Claude structures its thinking. Does it give you weeks? Resources? Does it feel like a plan, not just a list?

Step 2 — Try the same goal in ChatGPT

Go to → chatgpt.com

Paste the exact same prompt and compare.

"I want to start learning Spanish from scratch. I have 10 minutes a day. Please break this into a realistic 4-week plan: outline each week's focus, suggest one free resource for each week, and finish with a motivational note to keep me going."

Want to try your own goal instead? Here are some ideas:

  • Plan a weekend trip to a city you've never visited
  • Create a beginner fitness routine for someone with no equipment
  • Draft a 30-day reading plan for a book genre you want to explore
  • Prepare for a job interview in a field you're curious about
  • Build a simple budget plan for a month of saving
  • Design a meal plan for one week on a tight budget
  • Create a learning path for a hobby you've always wanted to try
👀What to
notice

Look at how each AI organised its response — not just what it said.

Did it create sections? Label them by week? Include resources without you asking for specific ones?

Notice that you gave one goal — and the AI built a structure around it. That's the "oh" moment here: you didn't manage the steps. The AI did.

That's what makes this different from a regular question.

💡Good to
know

"Agent" is a spectrum, not a switch.

The simple version you tried here is just a well-prompted conversation. More advanced agents — like the kind used in tools such as Make.com (Beacon 25) — can take actions, use external data, and run on their own. But the thinking is the same: one goal, multiple steps, AI handles the flow.

Gemini can do this too.

If you want a third perspective, try the same prompt in Gemini at gemini.google.com. All three tools — Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini — handle agent-style thinking well. It's worth seeing how each one structures its answer differently.

You're still the boss.

Agents don't replace your judgment. They handle the legwork so you can focus on the decisions that matter.
