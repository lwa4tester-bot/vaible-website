🧠 Look how far you've come

When you started this course, AI might have felt like something complicated. Something for tech experts. Something that wasn't really for you.

But you showed up. Beacon by Beacon.

You learned how to write with AI. How to think with it. How to create images, sounds, and videos. How to use it in your daily life — for emails, for planning, for ideas.

You didn't just learn about AI. You learned how to work with it.

🗺 ️ Your personal AI map

Every person leaves this course with a different set of favorites. That's exactly how it should be.

Some tools click immediately. Others feel useful but a bit tricky. A few might not have felt right for you at all — and that's fine too.

Now it's time to make that visible.

Open ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini — whichever feels most like home — and use this prompt:

🧪 Try it now

Copy and paste this prompt into your favorite AI tool:

Ready-made prompt
I've just finished an AI course where I tried tools like ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Pika, ElevenLabs, Sonauto, Leonardo AI, and others. I want to reflect on what I've learned. Please ask me 5 simple questions — one at a time — to help me figure out which tools fit my life best and how I might actually use AI going forward. Keep it friendly and relaxed.

Let the conversation unfold naturally. There are no wrong answers.

When you're done, ask the AI to summarize your answers in a few sentences — your personal AI snapshot.

👀 What to notice

Look at the summary the AI wrote about you.

Does it sound like you? Does it capture which tools felt useful and which ones didn't quite fit?

Notice this: an AI helped you reflect on AI. You're not just a student anymore — you're someone who knows how to use these tools to think, to plan, and to understand yourself better.

That's what this course was really about.

💡 Good to know

You don't have to use every tool. You don't have to remember everything.

The best AI habit isn't using the most tools — it's knowing which one or two feel natural to you, and reaching for them when it helps.

That's more than most people ever figure out.
